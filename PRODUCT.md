# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Visitors at the Maker Collective 2026 exhibition (CPF Makerspace × 42 Amman), standing in a bright, busy hall with a phone in one hand. Mostly Arabic speakers, some English; a mix of students, makers and families, on mid-range Android and iPhone, often on shared venue Wi-Fi or 4G. They have never seen this app and will use it once, for a few minutes, between booths. Secondary surfaces (not this brief): organisers on the admin console and the TV dashboard in the hall.

The job: prove you are really on site, then pick a favourite exhibitor in each award category and know, with certainty, that the vote was counted.

## Product Purpose

An on-site-only digital voting system for the event's award categories. One verified phone gets one final vote per category. Success is a visitor who finishes all categories without asking anyone for help, an organiser who trusts the count, and a pitch panel that sees both a polished experience and real access control.

## Positioning

The only voting experience at the event that is both effortless and trustworthy: you vote in seconds, and only people physically here, once each, can.

## Brand Personality

Confident, festive, tactile. Maker-workshop energy (the chevron, gear and triangle motifs from the brand guidelines) delivered with the polish of a premium native iOS app. Warm and direct in voice; Arabic is written as Arabic, not translated English. Satisfying but elegant motion: buttons that press down, smooth transitions, a rewarding animated finish when a vote lands.

## Anti-references

Generic SaaS templates (identical icon-and-text card grids, gradient text, glassy blurs, hero metrics). Government forms (dense, grey, errors that don't say what to do next). Casino or gamified UI (confetti everywhere, flashing badges, anything that makes a final vote feel like a game of chance). Cluttered Arabic afterthoughts (mirrored layouts with broken alignment, English-first UI with Arabic bolted on).

Positive references named by the owner: Careem for one-decision-per-screen flow and flawless Arabic-first RTL; Airbnb for clean photo-led cards and smooth bottom-sheet modals; the Apple Store iOS app for tactile polish, press-scale buttons, page transitions and a rewarding confirmation state.

## Design Principles

1. One decision per screen. The primary action sits in the thumb zone and the next step is always obvious.
2. Arabic is the first-class language. Layout, type and motion are designed in RTL first and mirrored for English, never the reverse.
3. Votes are final, so confirm deliberately and celebrate honestly. The UI never shows "voted" before the server has confirmed it.
4. Tactile, not toy. Every press, transition and success moment earns its place, stays fast, and stops when the user asks for reduced motion.
5. Never a dead end. Every refusal, error or offline state says what happened and what to do next.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Tap targets of at least 48px and inputs of at least 16px so iOS does not zoom. Text and controls meet the brand contrast table (white on purple, royal and crimson passes AA; navy text on yellow and turquoise; never yellow or turquoise as text on white). Every animation has a reduced-motion alternative. Haptics and sound are optional enhancements, never the only feedback. Targets: Lighthouse mobile Accessibility of at least 95 and Performance of at least 90, voter JavaScript under 150 KB gzipped.
